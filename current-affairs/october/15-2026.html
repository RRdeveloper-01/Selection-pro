Banking and Economy 15 October 2026

Government Approves Sale of 60.72% Stake in IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial

The Government of India approved the strategic sale of a combined 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank held by the government and the Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) to a company associated with Fairfax Financial Holdings.

According to the Ministry of Finance's published information, the transaction is valued at approximately ₹52,878 crore. The proposed stake sale includes 30.48% held by the Government of India and 30.24% held by LIC. The transaction remains subject to applicable regulatory requirements.

Key Points

  • Bank: IDBI Bank.
  • Strategic buyer's parent company: Fairfax Financial Holdings.
  • Total stake covered by the transaction: 60.72%.
  • Government of India stake included: 30.48%.
  • LIC stake included: 30.24%.
  • Reported transaction value: approximately ₹52,878 crore.
In One Line: The government approved the strategic sale of a combined 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank for approximately ₹52,878 crore.
Exam Fact: LIC stands for Life Insurance Corporation of India. The Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM) handles important disinvestment-related functions of the Government of India.

Source: Ministry of Finance / PIB | Reuters report

Oman Becomes India's Largest LNG Supplier in September 2026, According to Reports

The report linked the shift in import sources to disruptions affecting shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. Changes in energy suppliers can influence import logistics, energy security and the diversification of supply sources.

  • Reporting period: September 2026.
  • Previous leading supplier mentioned in the report: United States.

  • The Strait of Hormuz is an important route for global energy shipments.
In One Line: Oman reportedly replaced the United States as India's largest LNG supplier in September 2026.

Digital Payments and Economy 15 October 2026

Proposed MDR on Certain UPI Merchant Transactions Deferred Until After Diwali

The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) deferred the reported implementation of a proposed Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) for certain person-to-merchant (P2M) UPI transactions exceeding ₹2,000.

According to Reuters, the change had originally been expected to take effect on 15 October but was postponed until after Diwali 2026. A separate effective date was expected to be announced.

The proposal concerns certain merchant transactions and should not be interpreted as a fee applying automatically to every UPI payment. The final scope and applicable rules should be checked against official notifications.

Key Points

  • Organisation: National Payments Corporation of India.
  • MDR stands for Merchant Discount Rate.
  • The report concerned certain merchant transactions above ₹2,000.
  • The reported rollout was deferred until after Diwali.
  • A separate implementation date was expected to be announced.
In One Line: The proposed MDR for certain UPI merchant transactions above ₹2,000 was deferred until after Diwali.
Exam Fact: UPI stands for Unified Payments Interface. NPCI stands for National Payments Corporation of India.

Source: Official NPCI website | Read the Reuters report
