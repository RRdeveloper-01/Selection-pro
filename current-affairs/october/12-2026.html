PM inaugurates and dedicates projects worth over ₹13,000 crore in Chennai

Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s Chennai programme on 11 October featured the foundation stone laying, inauguration and dedication of development projects valued at more than ₹13,000 crore. The package covered urban transport, road connectivity, railway-station redevelopment and port-related infrastructure. The announcements are relevant to India news and infrastructure-focused current affairs because they connect public investment with mobility, logistics and regional economic activity.

Key points

  • The programme included work connected with Chennai Metro Rail Phase II, national highway projects and railway-station redevelopment.

  • The package also included port-related projects and the dedication of five Amrit Bharat stations.

Agriculture & EconomyUpdate: 12 October 2026

India’s Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses completes one year

The Government of India highlighted the first anniversary of the Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses, launched on 11 October 2025. The mission has a financial allocation of ₹11,440 crore for 2025–26 to 2030–31 and aims to strengthen domestic pulses production, reduce dependence on imports and support farmers through a broader value-chain approach.

Key points

  • The mission focuses on increasing domestic production and improving the availability of pulses.
  • NCCF and NAFED have been authorised to procure 100% of Tur, Masur and Urad under the stated arrangement for four years, from 2025–26 to 2028–29.
  • The government also highlighted seed traceability and digital agriculture tools, including the SATHI portal and AgriStack-linked initiatives.
In one line: India’s Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses completes one year
Exam Fact: Why it matters: Pulses are an important source of protein in Indian diets. Higher domestic production can support nutritional security, farm incomes and lower import exposure. Exam Fact: Mission allocation — ₹11,440 crore; mission period — 2025–26 to 2030–31.

International AffairsUpdate: 12 October 2026

IMF–World Bank meetings face pressure from conflict, debt and energy risks

Finance officials were gathering in Bangkok for the annual meetings of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank amid concerns about conflict-related energy disruption, inflation, higher interest rates and public debt. Reporting ahead of the meetings noted that these risks are particularly difficult for developing and low-income economies with large debt-service needs and limited fiscal space.

Key points

  • Energy supply disruptions can raise transport, production and food costs.
  • High interest rates can make borrowing and debt servicing more expensive.
  • Developing economies may have less room to fund health, education, infrastructure and climate resilience when debt payments rise.
In one line: IMF–World Bank meetings face pressure from conflict, debt and energy risks
Exam Fact: Why it matters: Global economic shocks can spread across borders through energy prices, trade, borrowing costs and investment. Exam Fact: The IMF and World Bank are separate international institutions; their annual meetings bring together finance and development policymakers.
