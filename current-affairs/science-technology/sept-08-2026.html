Science and Technology Current Affairs 8 September 2026: ISRO EOS-05 & Space News

Important Science and Technology Current Affairs for 8 September 2026, with a focus on ISRO's EOS-05 Earth observation mission, GSLV-F17, orbit-raising operations and India's changing space-sector ecosystem. Useful for UPSC, SSC, Railway, State PCS and interview preparation.

Published: 8 September 2026 · Category: Science & Technology · Exam-focused Current Affairs

1. ISRO completes third and final orbit-raising manoeuvre of EOS-05

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) successfully completed the third and final orbit-raising manoeuvre of the EOS-05 satellite at 8:57 pm on 7 September 2026.

According to ISRO, the Liquid Apogee Motor (LAM) engine fired for approximately 1,247 seconds. Following the manoeuvre, the estimated orbit was approximately 34,903 km × 35,884 km.

ISRO also reported that the spacecraft's health and functioning remained normal.

2. EOS-05 launched aboard GSLV-F17 on 4 September 2026

EOS-05 was launched on 4 September 2026 aboard India's GSLV-F17 launch vehicle.

EOS-05 is an Earth-observation spacecraft designed to provide imaging capabilities from geosynchronous orbit. The mission is significant because it expands India's Earth-observation capability in a geosynchronous orbit.

Interview Question: What is the primary purpose of an Earth-observation satellite?

Answer: Earth-observation satellites collect data and imagery about Earth's land, oceans and atmosphere for applications such as agriculture, disaster management, environmental monitoring and resource mapping.

3. EOS-05 becomes an important Indian geosynchronous Earth-observation mission

EOS-05 is significant for India's space programme because it is an Earth-imaging satellite intended to operate from geosynchronous orbit.

Geosynchronous satellites have orbital periods that match Earth's rotation period. Depending on their orbital inclination and other parameters, they can provide repeated coverage of specific regions.

Science Concept: A geostationary orbit is a special type of geosynchronous orbit in which a satellite appears fixed over one point on Earth's equator.

4. India's space sector reforms aim to increase private participation

India's space ecosystem is undergoing reforms designed to increase participation by private companies, startups and other non-government entities.

ISRO continues to remain central to advanced research, strategic missions and major national space capabilities, while policy reforms aim to create greater opportunities for the private sector.

Exam Connection: India's space reforms are linked with innovation, commercialisation of space activities, startups, technology development and public-private partnerships.

5. IN-SPACe plays a key role in India's private space ecosystem

The Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre (IN-SPACe) is the organisation responsible for promoting, enabling and regulating non-government entities in India's space activities.

IN-SPACe is intended to provide a framework through which private companies and startups can participate in space activities while using India's space infrastructure and capabilities under the applicable rules.

What is the difference between geosynchronous and geostationary orbit?

A geosynchronous satellite has an orbital period equal to Earth's rotation period. A geostationary orbit is a special geosynchronous orbit in which the satellite appears stationary over the equator.
