Science & Technology Current Affairs 9 September 2026: ISRO EOS-05 & Space Economy

Key science and technology news from India, with special focus on ISRO, EOS-05, satellite applications and the growth of India's space economy.

Published: 9 September 2026 · Exam-focused current affairs.

1. ISRO's EOS-05 settles into geosynchronous orbit

ISRO's Earth Observation Satellite EOS-05 successfully completed its final orbit-raising manoeuvre and entered geosynchronous orbit after its September 4 launch aboard GSLV-F17. The satellite is designed to provide continuous observation of the Indian subcontinent and support applications including weather and strategic imaging.

Exam Point: A geosynchronous satellite has an orbital period matching Earth's rotation, allowing it to remain nearly fixed relative to a region.

2. EOS-05 was launched on the GSLV-F17 mission

The GSLV-F17 mission placed the 2,360-kg EOS-05 satellite into an initial transfer orbit before subsequent manoeuvres moved it toward its operational orbit. The mission marked a major step in India's Earth-observation capability.

Exam Point: GSLV stands for Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle.

3. ISRO clarifies that it will not be privatised

ISRO has clarified that it will remain India's principal institution for advanced space research, strategic missions, space science and exploration. The clarification came amid discussion about transferring some mature manufacturing and operational activities to private or public-sector industry.

Exam Point: The policy direction is to expand the overall space ecosystem while retaining ISRO's central role in advanced and strategic missions.

4. India's private space ecosystem continues to expand

India's space reforms are encouraging greater participation by non-governmental entities, with IN-SPACe playing a key role in authorising and supporting private-sector space activities. Technology transfer and industry partnerships are intended to expand manufacturing and commercial capabilities.

Exam Point: IN-SPACe is the Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre.

5. EOS-05 can support weather, agriculture and disaster-management applications

Earth-observation satellites provide data that can be used for mapping, environmental monitoring, agriculture, disaster management and infrastructure planning. EOS-05's geosynchronous placement is particularly useful for repeated observation of the Indian region.

Exam Point: Remote sensing is the collection of information about Earth without direct physical contact with the target.

Quick Revision: Most Important Exam Points

  • EOS-05 was launched aboard GSLV-F17.
  • ISRO said it will remain India's principal institution for advanced and strategic space missions.
  • IN-SPACe supports non-governmental participation in India's space sector.
  • Earth-observation data can support agriculture, weather and disaster-management applications.
