1. SEMICON India 2026 Highlights India's Growing Semiconductor Ecosystem

What happened: SEMICON India 2026 concluded at Yashobhoomi, New Delhi, after bringing together companies, policymakers, researchers, startups and other participants from the global semiconductor ecosystem.

Why it matters: The event focused on chip design, semiconductor manufacturing, advanced packaging, research, equipment, supply chains and skilled talent.

Exam Fact: Semiconductors are essential components used in computers, smartphones, automobiles, communication systems, industrial equipment and AI infrastructure.

2. India Unveils Indigenous Vihaan-I Broadband Networking Chip

What happened: Chennai-based Aheesa Digital Innovations unveiled Vihaan-I at SEMICON India 2026. The broadband networking chip was designed in India and is based on the open-standard RISC-V architecture.

Why it matters: The development represents India's push towards indigenous chip design for broadband, telecom and edge-computing applications and supports the wider objective of reducing dependence on imported technology.

Exam Fact: RISC-V is an open-standard Instruction Set Architecture used as a foundation for designing processors.

3. Applied Materials Plans $5 Billion India Investment

What happened: US semiconductor-equipment company Applied Materials announced plans to invest about $5 billion in India over the next decade.

Why it matters: The planned investment is expected to support research, supply-chain development and workforce expansion as India develops its semiconductor ecosystem.

Exam Fact: Semiconductor equipment is used in different stages of chip manufacturing and processing, making equipment suppliers an important part of the semiconductor value chain.

4. India Semiconductor Market Could Reach $200 Billion by 2035

What happened: An EY-IESA report projects India's semiconductor market could expand from around $64 billion in 2026 to about $200 billion by 2035.

Why it matters: Growth is expected to be supported by consumer electronics, automobiles, industrial applications, artificial intelligence, data centres, electric mobility and telecommunications.

Exam Fact: India has a significant share of the world's semiconductor design talent, making chip design an important strength of the country's technology ecosystem.

5. Semicon 2.0 Expands India's Chip Ecosystem Strategy

What happened: India's semiconductor strategy is moving beyond individual fabrication projects towards a wider ecosystem covering chip design, fabrication, advanced packaging, equipment, materials, research and skilled talent.

Why it matters: A broader semiconductor value chain can strengthen India's electronics manufacturing capabilities and improve resilience in global technology supply chains.

Exam Fact: OSAT stands for Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test, an important stage of semiconductor packaging and testing.
