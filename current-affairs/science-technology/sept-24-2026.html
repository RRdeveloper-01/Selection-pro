1. India Strengthens Semiconductor Ecosystem Through SEMICON India 2026

What happened: SEMICON India 2026 brought together companies, researchers, policymakers and technology stakeholders to strengthen India's semiconductor and electronics ecosystem. The event focused on semiconductor manufacturing, AI, advanced materials, supply chains, product design and innovation.

Why it matters: Semiconductors are essential for smartphones, computers, automobiles, defence systems, artificial intelligence and many other modern technologies. Building a domestic semiconductor ecosystem can strengthen India's technology and manufacturing capabilities.

Exam Fact: SEMICON India 2026 was organised under the theme “Silicon to Systems: Building the Ecosystem.”

2. ISRO and ESA Renew Space Cooperation Until 2032

What happened: The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and European Space Agency (ESA) have renewed their cooperation agreement until 2032.

Why it matters: The renewed cooperation covers areas including space weather and robotic space exploration. International collaboration can help improve scientific research, technology development and future space missions.

Exam Fact: ISRO is India's national space agency, while ESA is an intergovernmental organisation dedicated to the exploration of space.

3. Indian Railways Commissions KAVACH 4.0 in Hyderabad Division

What happened: Indian Railways has successfully commissioned KAVACH Version 4.0 for the first time on the South Central Railway's Hyderabad Division.

Why it matters: KAVACH is an indigenous automatic train protection system designed to improve railway safety by helping prevent collisions and supporting safer train operations.

Exam Fact: KAVACH is India's indigenous Automatic Train Protection (ATP) system.

4. AI Infrastructure Becomes a Major Technology Priority

What happened: Artificial intelligence infrastructure has become a major focus of discussions around India's digital and technology ecosystem. AI expansion requires advanced computing, semiconductor capacity, data centres and reliable energy infrastructure.

Why it matters: The growth of AI is increasing demand for high-performance computing and advanced chips. This connects AI policy with semiconductor manufacturing, electricity supply and digital infrastructure.

Exam Fact: AI infrastructure includes computing systems, data centres, networks, specialised processors and supporting energy infrastructure.

5. AI-Driven Cybersecurity Threats Draw Global Attention

What happened: New cybersecurity assessments are highlighting how artificial intelligence can increase the speed and sophistication of cyber threats, prompting organisations to strengthen their cyber resilience.

Why it matters: As AI tools become more capable, cybersecurity strategies increasingly need to address both AI-enabled threats and the secure use of AI systems.

Exam Fact: Cyber resilience refers to an organisation's ability to prepare for, withstand, respond to and recover from cyber incidents.
