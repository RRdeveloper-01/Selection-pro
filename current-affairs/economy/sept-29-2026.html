1. Rupee Falls as Higher Oil Prices Pressure the Currency

The Indian rupee weakened to a more than one-week low as rising crude-oil prices increased pressure on the currency. Higher oil prices matter for India because the country imports a large share of its crude requirement, affecting import costs, inflation and the external sector.

Exam Fact: Crude oil is one of India's major import commodities.

2. UPI Pricing Debate Keeps Digital Payments in Focus

The Supreme Court's decision not to stay the Centre's UPI Merchant Discount Rate decision has brought the economics of digital payments back into focus. The issue concerns how payment-system costs are handled for eligible UPI transactions.

Exam Fact: MDR stands for Merchant Discount Rate.

3. Government Borrowing Remains Important for Fiscal Management

Government borrowing through securities is an important way of financing the fiscal deficit. The borrowing programme affects the government-securities market, liquidity conditions and the wider interest-rate environment.

Exam Fact: Fiscal deficit represents the gap between government expenditure and receipts excluding borrowings.

4. Investment and Trade Facilitation Remain Policy Priorities

India continues to work on measures intended to facilitate trade and investment, including improvements in customs procedures and investment-treaty engagement. Such measures can reduce transaction costs and support global supply-chain integration.

Exam Fact: CBIC is the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs.

5. Oil Prices and the External Sector

Changes in international crude prices have a direct bearing on India's import bill. When oil becomes more expensive, the value of imports can rise and place pressure on the current account and the rupee.

Exam Fact: The current account is a component of India's balance of payments.
