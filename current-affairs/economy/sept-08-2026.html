Economy Current Affairs 8 September 2026: RBI, Forex Reserves & Banking News

Important Economy Current Affairs for 8 September 2026, covering RBI liquidity management, India's foreign-exchange reserves, rupee movement, FCNR(B) deposits and banking-sector developments. These topics are useful for UPSC, SSC, Banking, Railway, State PCS and interview preparation.

Published: 8 September 2026 · Category: Economy · Exam-focused Current Affairs

1. RBI absorbs ₹6 lakh crore excess liquidity from banking system

The Reserve Bank of India absorbed more than ₹6 lakh crore of excess liquidity from the banking system through two Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions on 7 September 2026.

The move came after a large amount of foreign-currency inflows entered India's banking system through the special forex mobilisation measures. The RBI is using liquidity management operations to prevent excess funds from creating instability in short-term money markets.

Exam Point: VRRR is a liquidity-management tool through which the RBI absorbs surplus funds from banks for a specified period.

2. India's foreign-exchange reserves reach record $740.8 billion

India's foreign-exchange reserves reached a record $740.8 billion for the week ended 28 August 2026.

According to reported RBI data, foreign currency assets increased significantly while India's gold reserves also rose. The increase provides India with a stronger external financial buffer against global economic and financial shocks.

Exam Point: India's forex reserves consist mainly of foreign-currency assets, gold reserves, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) and India's reserve position with the IMF.

3. RBI sells dollars to support the Indian rupee

The Reserve Bank of India reportedly sold at least $8 billion in the foreign-exchange market during the previous week, with bankers estimating the intervention could have been as high as $15 billion.

The intervention helped the Indian rupee strengthen to around ₹94.2850 per US dollar on 3 September, its strongest level in more than two months at that time.

Why is this important? RBI intervention in the forex market can influence both the exchange rate and rupee liquidity in the banking system.

4. Special forex measures attract more than $136 billion

RBI's special foreign-exchange mobilisation measures attracted more than $136 billion in inflows by 31 August 2026.

A major component came through Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank), or FCNR(B), deposits. RBI data reported FCNR(B) inflows of about $127.23 billion.

Exam Point: FCNR(B) stands for Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank). These are foreign-currency deposits maintained by eligible non-residents with authorised banks in India.

5. Surplus liquidity becomes an important RBI policy challenge

Large foreign-currency inflows have contributed to a sharp increase in liquidity in India's banking system. The surplus has reached levels that require active management by the central bank.

Excess liquidity can push short-term money-market rates below the policy repo rate and can make monetary-policy transmission more difficult. The RBI can therefore use instruments such as VRRR operations and other liquidity management measures.

Interview Question: Why does the RBI absorb excess liquidity?
Answer: To maintain orderly money-market conditions, support monetary-policy transmission and reduce the risks created by excessive liquidity.

Economy Current Affairs FAQs

What are the most important economy current affairs of 8 September 2026?

Key topics include RBI's liquidity absorption, India's record $740.8 billion forex reserves, RBI's dollar intervention and large FCNR(B) inflows.

What is FCNR(B)?

FCNR(B) stands for Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank). It refers to eligible non-resident deposits maintained in foreign currency with authorised banks in India.

What is VRRR in banking?

Variable Rate Reverse Repo, or VRRR, is an RBI liquidity-management operation used to absorb surplus funds from the banking system.

Why are India's forex reserves important?

Forex reserves provide a financial buffer against external shocks, support confidence in the economy and give the RBI greater flexibility in managing foreign-exchange market conditions.
