1. Rupee Closes at ₹95.96 per Dollar

What happened: The Indian rupee weakened to around ₹95.96 against the US dollar on 24 September amid importer dollar demand, higher crude prices and global bond-yield pressures.

Why it matters: The exchange rate matters for India's import bill, inflation and external-sector conditions.

Exam Fact: A higher rupee-per-dollar figure indicates a weaker rupee against the US dollar.

2. Centre Cuts Import Duties on Edible Oils

What happened: The Centre has reduced import duties on edible oils ahead of the festive season, according to reports.

Why it matters: Lower import duties can affect domestic availability and prices of imported edible oils.

Exam Fact: Edible oils are important food commodities and form part of India's agricultural and trade policy.

3. RBI Deputy Governor Says Rupee Can Stabilise

What happened: RBI Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said there is a fair case for the rupee to stabilise and appreciate from current levels.

Why it matters: The comment comes amid attention to crude prices, capital flows, inflation and foreign-exchange conditions.

Exam Fact: The RBI manages India's monetary policy and foreign-exchange reserves.

4. Crude Oil Remains a Key External Risk

What happened: Rising crude oil prices continue to influence India's currency and external economic outlook.

Why it matters: India imports a large share of its crude-oil requirement, so global oil prices can affect trade, inflation and the rupee.

Exam Fact: Crude oil is one of India's major import commodities.

5. Global Bond Yields and Dollar Demand Pressure the Rupee

What happened: Global bond yields and strong importer demand for dollars have added pressure on the Indian currency.

Why it matters: Changes in global financial conditions can affect capital flows and exchange rates in emerging markets.

Exam Fact: Bond yields represent the return investors receive from holding bonds.
