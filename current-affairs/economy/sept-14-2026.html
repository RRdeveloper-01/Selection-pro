1. Crude oil rises above $100 as Middle East supply risks deepen

International oil markets are facing renewed supply pressure amid the Iran conflict, disruption around key shipping routes and attacks affecting Saudi oil infrastructure. Higher crude prices are especially important for India because of its dependence on imported oil.

Exam Point: Exam Point: Higher crude prices can raise India's import bill and inflationary pressure.

2. Rupee remains under pressure from oil and global yields

The Indian rupee has faced pressure as crude prices rise and global bond yields remain elevated. Recent reporting pointed to a sharp weekly decline and signs of RBI intervention.

Exam Point: Exam Point: Key factors include crude oil, dollar strength, foreign capital flows and global yields.

3. RBI uses market operations amid rupee pressure

The Reserve Bank of India has been reported to use foreign-exchange operations including dollar-rupee swaps as pressure on the currency increased.

Exam Point: Exam Point: RBI uses monetary and foreign-exchange tools to support financial and market stability.

4. BRICS supports local-currency trade and cross-border payments

The BRICS declaration supported stronger trade in local currencies and improved cross-border payment cooperation.

Exam Point: Exam Point: Important keywords: local currencies, cross-border payments and trade diversification.

5. India's economic outlook faces an energy-price challenge

Persistent energy-price volatility can affect India's inflation, current account, transport costs and business sentiment, making energy security an important economic-policy issue.

Exam Point: Exam Point: Connect crude oil with inflation, current account, import bill and rupee pressure.
