1. Rupee Trades Under Pressure as Crude Oil Prices Remain Important

The Indian rupee has remained under pressure as crude oil and the US dollar influence currency markets. Recent trading saw the rupee near the ₹96-per-dollar level, while oil prices remained an important factor for traders and policymakers.

Exam Fact: India imports a large share of its crude-oil requirement.

2. Crude Oil Prices Remain a Key Market Driver

Global crude prices remain important because changes in oil costs can affect India's import bill, inflation and external sector. Market movements are also influenced by expectations around Middle East supply and exports.

Exam Fact: Brent crude is an international oil-price benchmark.

3. RBI Intervention Helps Limit Sharp Rupee Moves

Recent currency-market trading has seen state-run banks selling dollars, reportedly helping limit the rupee's decline. Such activity is part of the wider foreign-exchange framework in which the RBI can act to manage excessive volatility.

Exam Fact: RBI stands for Reserve Bank of India.

4. Digital Payment Economics Remains in Focus

Recent discussions around UPI merchant charges have brought attention to the economics of India's digital-payment ecosystem and how payment costs are distributed among banks, payment providers and merchants.

Exam Fact: MDR stands for Merchant Discount Rate.

5. India's External Sector Remains Sensitive to Oil Prices

When crude oil becomes more expensive, India's import bill can rise and put pressure on the trade balance and rupee. The final effect also depends on exports, capital flows and other global conditions.

Exam Fact: The current account is a component of the balance of payments.
