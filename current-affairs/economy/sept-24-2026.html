1. RBI Deputy Governor Says Rupee Has Scope to Stabilise

What happened: RBI Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said the Indian rupee has scope to stabilise and potentially appreciate from current levels, according to recent remarks.

Why it matters: The rupee's movement is closely watched because exchange-rate changes affect import costs, inflation, external payments and overall financial-market conditions.

Exam Fact: The Reserve Bank of India is India's central bank and is responsible for monetary policy and management of foreign-exchange reserves.

2. RBI Approves Banking Operations Ahead of Proposed Strike

What happened: The Reserve Bank of India has approved Public Sector Banks and Regional Rural Banks to remain fully operational on Sunday, 27 September 2026.

Why it matters: The decision is aimed at maintaining continuity of banking and payment services ahead of a proposed three-day nationwide bank strike from 28 to 30 September.

Exam Fact: Regional Rural Banks were established to improve credit and banking access in rural areas.

3. India's Private Sector Activity Shows Stronger Growth

What happened: Latest business-survey indicators showed an improvement in India's private-sector activity, with the composite business activity indicator reaching a three-month high.

Why it matters: Private-sector activity indicators provide an early signal about demand, output, business confidence and employment conditions in the economy.

Exam Fact: A Purchasing Managers' Index, or PMI, is a widely used indicator of business activity based on survey responses.

4. BRICS Tax Cooperation Gains Importance

What happened: India has continued to emphasise stronger tax cooperation among BRICS countries and the need for international tax arrangements to reflect changing economic realities.

Why it matters: Cross-border taxation has become increasingly important as international trade, digital businesses and global investment flows expand.

Exam Fact: BRICS is a grouping of major emerging economies that promotes cooperation in areas including finance, trade and development.

5. Crude Oil and Global Geopolitics Remain Important for Rupee

What happened: Global crude-oil prices and geopolitical developments continue to influence the Indian rupee and India's external economic outlook.

Why it matters: India imports a large share of its crude-oil requirement. Higher oil prices can increase the import bill and put pressure on inflation, the current account and the rupee.

Exam Fact: Crude oil is one of India's major import commodities, making global oil prices important for the Indian economy.
