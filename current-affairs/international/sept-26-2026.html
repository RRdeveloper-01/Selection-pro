1. Iran Proposes Seven-Day Roadmap to Reopen Strait of Hormuz

What happened: Iran has proposed a plan under which the strategically important Strait of Hormuz could be reopened within seven days if the United States lifts its naval blockade and meets other conditions. Iran has also linked the proposal with resuming negotiations on its nuclear programme.

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most important energy-shipping routes. Any disruption can have implications for global oil supplies, shipping and international markets.

Exam Fact: The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea.

2. Iran Says It Will Not Make Nuclear Concessions

What happened: An Iranian official said Iran would not make concessions on its nuclear programme even if the United States accepted Tehran's proposal concerning the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: Iran's nuclear programme remains a major issue in international diplomacy and is closely connected with regional security and negotiations involving the United States.

Exam Fact: The International Atomic Energy Agency, or IAEA, is the international organisation associated with nuclear safeguards and peaceful uses of nuclear energy.

3. 80 Countries Call for Restoration of Gulf Maritime Navigation

What happened: A group of 80 countries called at the United Nations for the restoration of maritime navigation in the Gulf amid continuing disruption around the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: Safe maritime navigation is important for international trade, energy transport and the stability of global supply chains.

Exam Fact: Freedom of navigation is an important principle in international maritime law and global trade.

4. Lebanon Seeks to Avoid Becoming an Arena for Regional Conflicts

What happened: Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam told Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian that Lebanon did not want to become an arena for regional conflicts during their meeting on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly.

Why it matters: Lebanon's position is relevant to wider West Asia diplomacy because regional tensions can affect security, political stability and economic conditions across neighbouring countries.

Exam Fact: Lebanon is located on the eastern Mediterranean coast and shares borders with Syria and Israel.

5. UN General Assembly 81st Session Continues Global Diplomacy

What happened: The high-level week of the 81st United Nations General Assembly continues to bring world leaders together to discuss conflicts, development, climate, technology, international security and multilateral cooperation.

Why it matters: The UN General Assembly is a central forum for international diplomacy, where all UN member states have an opportunity to participate in discussions on global issues.

Exam Fact: The United Nations General Assembly has 193 member states.
